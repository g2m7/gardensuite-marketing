# Website, inbound content and paid discovery

Priority: website P1; Meta paid P3; Google/LinkedIn paid P4 | Owner: Kaushik with project maintainer

## Goal

Give interested owners a credible place to understand the smaller package and a private path to enquire. Buy distribution only when the offer and capture flow are usable and the test has a spending ceiling.

## Website and organic inbound setup

1. Inspect current attendance and MIS pages against the standalone offer. Show exact included reports and data inputs; do not imply the full ERP is required if it is not.
2. Add the owner check as an optional route while retaining direct Book Free Demo and Email Us actions.
3. Show a real sample demonstration, support identity and a clear explanation of offline capture/later sync. Use only approved proof.
4. Verify production contact forms and acknowledgement delivery. Do not assume an existing component or old Brevo integration is operating correctly.
5. Label source paths and capture outcomes privately. Review which existing enquiries came from search, social, referrals or previous contact before investing in new content.
6. Create short practical content from owner questions: checking incomplete daily records, seeing corrections, or reviewing attendance away from the garden. Avoid a large generic SEO programme before demand is measured.

Any new public route must pass the complete AGENTS.md new-page checklist and the current SEO/copy/typography/styling guides. Include canonical, social previews, true schema, sitemap, internal links, one H1, accessible mobile layout, image/performance requirements and Sarbani Associates. The old brochure gate remains historical; this is a new micro-magnet specification.

## Meta paid experiment

Prerequisites: owner validation passed, page/form works, lead quality can be checked, response owner available, geography and test budget selected.

1. Set up the business/page/ad account identity, billing owner and access. Confirm the current ad account supports the intended lead/website objective and conversion event.
2. Start with one narrowly stated owner problem and two creative versions at most: report completeness versus attendance corrections. Use sample screens and a truthful invitation.
3. Use available geographic controls consistent with the agreed market. Estate location and owner residence may differ; a district-only audience is a test hypothesis and may miss owners based elsewhere. Ask the estate location during qualification rather than assuming the ad's location proves it.
4. For the first test, send people to the mobile owner check. A native Instant Form can be compared later using the same offer and role/estate questions. Meta's [lead-form guide](https://about.fb.com/ltam/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2023/11/LeadGenerationGuide.pdf) describes forms and qualifying questions. Actual current UI availability must be inspected before setup.
5. Record objective, audience, placements, start/end, event, two creatives, total loss cap and review date before activation.
6. Export/match qualified estate outcomes daily during a paid test. Optimizing only for cheap submissions can hide an irrelevant audience.

Do not promise selection of another group's members as a paid audience, scrape members for uploads, or use the old private-number list for ad audiences. The public Meta targeting help page was login-blocked during this research; current controls must be checked directly before any paid launch. No group-targeting workaround is part of this plan.

Retargeting may be considered for GardenSuite's own eligible visitors/engagers after measurement and permission handling are verified. Do not assume the audience is large enough or upload a contact list without the required permission.

## Budget and stop rules

No rupee budget is assumed. Set a test loss cap B before launch and a review point no later than fourteen days. Total account/campaign controls must prevent unintended spend beyond B; monitor delivered spend because pacing can differ by platform.

Track cost per verified owner estate, cost per completed demo and acquisition cost per paid installation. Maximum affordable acquisition cost comes from entry-package contribution and the acceptable payback period, not the hoped-for ERP upgrade.

At half the budget or seven days, whichever comes first, inspect audience quality and the form. If ten reviewed submissions contain fewer than three genuine in-scope owner/director accounts, revise/stop the current audience or qualification approach. This is an internal diagnostic rule, not an industry benchmark. If volume is too small, report insufficient evidence rather than declaring a winner.

Stop on budget exhaustion, broken capture, unresolved data exposure, misleading creative or a platform warning. Do not increase spend automatically after cheap leads.

## Google Search ads

Later only. Use current keyword planning and actual query data to establish whether owners search for tea garden attendance, face attendance or tea garden MIS. Keep consumer tea, tea shopping, tourism, jobs and irrelevant generic office attendance out of the test. Use a few tightly relevant keywords, dedicated relevant landing copy and a defined spend cap. Do not invent search volume or buy broad generic ERP traffic without evidence.

## LinkedIn ads

Later only. See the [LinkedIn guide](../05-linkedin/README.md) for audience minimum and targeting-source checks. Do not commit budget if the reachable audience or expected economics is inadequate. A larger regional test needs a deliberate scope decision.

## Do / do not

Do keep direct enquiry available, verify production forms, match the ad to the offered result, and use owner-specific language. Do not claim theft, fabricated loss amounts, fake testimonials, guaranteed returns or automatic real-time data. Do not assume that people interested in tea are estate owners.

## Goals and acceptance criteria

- [ ] Existing direct enquiries route to a responsible person and receive an acknowledgement.
- [ ] Public smaller-package claims are technically verified.
- [ ] New pages pass the full repository launch requirements.
- [ ] Any paid test has final audience/settings/creative/spend cap and user authorization.
- [ ] No private contact data is exposed in URLs, event payloads or public screenshots.
- [ ] Leads are matched to distinct verified estates; qualified conversations and demos are reported alongside costs.
- [ ] Paid expansion is based on lead quality and delivery economics, with sufficient service capacity.
