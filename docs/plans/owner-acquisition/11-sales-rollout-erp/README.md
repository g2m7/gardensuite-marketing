# Sales, rollout, retention and later ERP expansion

Priority: ongoing from first interest | Accountable: Kaushik | Delivery: named Sarbani Associates implementation lead

## Goal

Convert a requested conversation into a useful working attendance/reporting installation, then expand only where the estate has a demonstrated need.

## Qualification and demonstration

1. Confirm estate, role, decision-maker, current process and the one reporting problem the buyer wants to solve. Ask who approves the purchase and who will implement it.
2. Establish the standalone fit: worker masters, devices, field conditions, office review, owner access and the required report inputs. Existing software is a qualification topic, not an automatic rejection.
3. Run a 15-20 minute demonstration with sample records. Show an offline field entry, sync/review and the report as the owner sees it. Mark any simulated or future functionality clearly.
4. Address the buyer's specific gap. A supportive manager can join implementation planning; direct owner interest does not justify excluding operational staff needed for success.
5. Agree the next action and date before the meeting ends. Send the concise scope and quote requested, not a full-ERP deck by default.

## Quote and trial setup

Use the foundation scope/price worksheet. Record included features, devices, reports, installation/training, recurring charges, travel, support and payment terms. No invented public price or savings promise.

If a pilot is commercially useful, define its duration, section/workers, data handling, responsibilities, price/deposit, acceptance and end decision. Do not promise unlimited free trials. Credit toward a larger rollout only if deliberately priced and included in the quotation.

## Installation acceptance

Agree site-specific values before starting. Suggested acceptance areas:

- Representative workers can be enrolled and attendance recorded on the selected devices.
- Offline capture and later transfer are demonstrated in the actual environment.
- Office staff can review corrections and distinguish pending from complete records.
- Owner can access the included reports and understands their update timing and limitations.
- Export/current-system fit is demonstrated for the agreed scope.
- Staff training completed and support contact/process provided.
- Site-specific fallback, incident and escalation process explained.

These are acceptance areas, not promises of universal recognition accuracy or a fixed implementation time. Record the actual worker sample, required success conditions and remaining exceptions with the buyer.

## Retention routine

Plan reviews around day 7 and day 30 after working use begins. Inspect adoption, record completeness, unresolved support issues and whether the owner is actually using the report. Fix unresolved delivery problems before requesting a referral or proposing more modules.

Record support effort and direct delivery cost. If the small package takes more support than the price covers, adjust onboarding/scope/pricing before acquiring more of the same work.

## ERP expansion triggers

Offer the next module when a confirmed need appears: repeated payroll entry, disconnected stores records or factory reporting gaps. Use the estate's own stated problem and demonstrate that module's specific fit.

At a 30-60 day value review, ask which remaining task takes the most effort. Prepare a separate scope and quote when there is a need and budget. Carry forward compatible data and agreed commercial credits where supported; do not promise migration compatibility without verification.

The owner must be able to continue using the attendance product without buying the full ERP. Do not withhold basic support or essential reports to force the upgrade.

## Do / do not

Do record the buying role, deliver a clear scope and solve the first workflow completely. Do not sell the full ERP before the smaller need is understood, use the self-check as proof of losses, manufacture urgency, promise unsupported integration or push upgrades during unresolved support problems.

## Goals and acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every qualified demo has source, estate, role, need, package and next action.
- [ ] Every paid installation has a signed/accepted scope and site-specific acceptance record.
- [ ] First-week and first-month reviews are scheduled.
- [ ] Owner can use the promised daily report and staff know the support route.
- [ ] Entry-product contribution and support effort are measured.
- [ ] Referral request follows delivered value and customer willingness.
- [ ] ERP proposals cite an actual remaining need and carry a separate price/scope.

Report demo-to-quote, quote-to-paid, installation acceptance, retained use and ERP expansion separately. No 50% conversion forecast until the measured source-specific sample supports it.
