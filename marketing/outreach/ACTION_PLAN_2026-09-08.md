## Locked decisions

- Geography: Dibrugarh and Tinsukia districts only.
- Pilot size: 20 estate accounts, one primary contact per estate.
- Preferred estate: mid-tier, roughly 250 workers, at least 50 hectares, independently managed or part of a small family group.
- Avoid: very large or prominent estates, public companies, large multi-estate groups, current GardenSuite clients, suppressed accounts, and active sales discussions.
- Existing or older software is not an automatic rejection. It can indicate a replacement opportunity.
- Primary roles: owner, founder, director, managing director, general manager, estate manager, and garden manager.
- Primary offer: face-verified hazira, offline field use, office review, installation, training, and support by Sarbani Associates.
- Smart weighing may be mentioned when it fits the estate's workflow.
- Sending platform: Snov.io Campaigns.
- Warm-up: use Snov.io automated warm-up through 2026-09-09. The existing start remains 2 messages, with a 1-message daily increase and a 4-message daily goal.
- Keep Snov.io warm-up reply rate at 30% and pause automated warm-up before prospect sending begins on 2026-09-10.
- Daily preparation: the `GardenSuite September outbound` Codex heartbeat remains scheduled at 12:00 Asia/Kolkata through 2026-09-14.
- First email: plain text, no attachment, no brochure, no image, no video, and no calendar link.
- Every cold address must return `Valid` from Snov.io.

## Sending and stop rules

- Stop the sequence immediately on reply, unsubscribe, hard bounce, spam complaint, or manual suppression.
- Pause the entire pilot on any hard bounce, spam complaint, provider warning, or repeated Spam-folder placement.
- Do not contact a second person at the same estate while the first contact is active in the sequence.
- Do not attach marketing material to the first cold email.
- Do not invent a personal detail, worker count, software problem, or nearby client reference.
- Do not name a client without permission. Use region-level proof when permission is absent.
- Reply to relevant responses manually and promptly.

## Facebook group plan

### Goal

Use groups to find and begin conversations with tea estate owners, managers, directors, and planters. Do not use groups as a place for repeated sales posts.

### Group discovery searches

- `Assam tea planters`
- `tea estate managers Assam`
- `Dibrugarh tea industry`
- `Tinsukia tea gardens`
- `tea garden owners Assam`
- `tea plantation professionals India`

### Group qualification

Join only when the group has recent posts, relevant members, understandable rules, and real participation from tea-industry professionals. Consumer tea groups, tourist groups, inactive groups, and groups dominated by unrelated jobs are not priority channels.

### Organic routine

- Use Kaushik's real profile with GardenSuite and Sarbani Associates shown clearly.
- Read the group rules before posting or messaging.
- During the first week, add useful comments to relevant discussions. Do not lead with a website link or demo request.
- Publish no more than one discussion post per group per week.
- Write a separate version for each group. Do not paste the same post everywhere.
- Continue a useful public conversation before asking permission to send a private message.
- Log qualified people and estate accounts in the canonical tracker before outreach.

Suggested discussion post:

> For tea estates using biometric or face hazira, which causes more difficulty: weak internet in the field, worker enrolment, or checking attendance before payroll?

Suggested permission-based private message:

> Thank you for sharing that. I work with GardenSuite at Sarbani Associates. We handle offline face attendance and office review for tea gardens. Would it be useful if I sent a short explanation of the workflow?

### Facebook paid targeting

If paid testing is approved later, use Dibrugarh and Tinsukia geography, tea-estate-specific creative, and retarget people who engage with GardenSuite's own Page, video, website, or lead form. Verify current options in Meta's official targeting documentation before launch.

## LinkedIn group and profile plan

### Organic routine

- Search for the same tea-industry terms used for Facebook groups.
- Target Owner, Founder, Director, Managing Director, General Manager, Estate Manager, Garden Manager, Tea Planter, and Plantation Manager roles.
- Use group membership to discover people, then verify their present estate relationship through their profile and company sources.
- Send up to 5 relevant connection requests per day.
- Do not pitch in the connection request.
- Interact with relevant posts before sending a product message where practical.
- Create a group-specific post instead of sharing or duplicating one post across groups.

Suggested connection note:

> Hello {{first_name}}, I work with GardenSuite at Sarbani Associates. I follow practical tea-garden operations in Assam and would be glad to connect.

Suggested message after relevant engagement:

> Thank you for connecting. We help tea gardens record face-verified hazira offline and review it in the office before payroll. Is attendance or leaf-weight recording something you handle at {{estate_name}}?

### LinkedIn paid targeting

LinkedIn currently supports Member Groups targeting in India, but an ad audience must meet LinkedIn's minimum size. Combining a small tea group with only Dibrugarh and Tinsukia may be too narrow. Verify the platform rules before any paid launch.

For this pilot, use organic profile outreach instead of paid LinkedIn ads. Consider paid testing only after the email and organic messages identify a proven response angle and the reachable audience exceeds the current platform minimum.

## Social measurement

Track business outcomes, not likes alone:

- qualified estate accounts discovered
- verified owner or manager profiles found
- accepted relevant connections
- useful public conversations
- permission-based private conversations
- positive replies
- demo requests
- suppressions or negative feedback

Review social activity weekly. Stop using a group when posts are repeatedly removed, administrators object, or the audience is not the target market.

## Pilot success

The pilot succeeds when it produces at least 2 positive, relevant replies or at least 1 qualified demo from the 20 accounts, with no suppression failure.

After the sequences finish, record positive replies, neutral replies, objections, no responses, bounces, opt-outs, demos, and lessons. Do not increase volume automatically.
