# GardenSuite owner access review

Date: 6 November 2026
Status: Recommendation for discussion. This does not replace CURRENT_STRATEGY.md or authorize sending, spending or campaign activation.
Prepared for GardenSuite, Sarbani Associates, Bagdogra.

## Recommendation

Spend the next month testing introductions that produce an agreed owner meeting. Give the introducer and the estate office a short visual demonstration they can forward. Use LinkedIn and relevant Facebook groups to find both buyers and people who know them.

The commercial route to test is:

Known tea adviser or customer -> agreed introduction -> sample demonstration -> small records review -> scoped 30-day shadow deployment.

An introduction counts when the buyer agrees to speak. A name, private number, delivered brochure or promise to mention us does not count.

This is a hypothesis with some support from the recorded referral sale. It is not a proven acquisition system yet.

## What the review established

- The owner reports that cold outreach is failing, offices repeatedly defer visits, and staff do not give out owners' personal contacts.
- The owner says brochures, social campaigns and LinkedIn have not yet been tried. Existing playbooks are preparation, not evidence of execution.
- CURRENT_STRATEGY.md records no September email replies and one referral-led sale. The live Snov campaign and mailbox were not inspected for this review; September counts are not November results.
- The action plan sets eight attended visits and four demos a week. It does not establish how a salesperson will get those appointments. Given the owner's experience, that assumption needs testing before more travel or a hiring commitment.
- The referral plan lists plausible partners, but its target of ten signed partners can conceal a lack of introductions. Completed buyer conversations should lead the scorecard.
- The earlier owner-acquisition decisions record failed long association mail circulation. Another forwarded marketing email is weak grounds for renewed effort.
- The product strategy already proposes a Tea Loss Audit and a 30-day shadow deployment. The acquisition gap is getting someone to consider those offers.

## 1. Find an introducer with demonstrated access

Start through existing customer relationships. Ask which independent advisers, visiting agents, scale-service businesses or other providers already discuss operational purchases with their owner. A supplier who visits a store may have no access to the person who approves software.

Suggested customer ask:

> When you last bought equipment or changed a garden process, whose advice did you take? Would you be comfortable introducing us to that person?

Assess each candidate against three questions:

1. Which eligible independent estates do they work with now?
2. Do they speak with the owner about purchasing decisions?
3. Can they arrange one introduction where the owner has agreed to a short conversation?

Start with one estate per candidate. Keep North Bengal and Assam equally represented in the effort. Hold early meetings remotely where travel is difficult.

If hiring, assess the person's current relationships as carefully as their sales experience. A retired manager or visiting adviser may fit, but the title alone proves little. A new salesperson facing the same reception desk may reproduce the same result.

Commission amounts remain open in the authority file. Do not quote the example amounts as approved terms. Any paid referral needs written terms and payment only after the estate pays. Do not pay the buying estate's staff without the owner's written knowledge. Auditors or advisers with conflicts may be unsuitable for commission arrangements.

## 2. Make a brochure staff can forward

Prepare a two-page sales brief, printable front and back, and a short demonstration video. The brief should make sense when forwarded without an explanation from Kaushik.

Page one should show the current working process: face-verified hazira, leaf weight linked to the worker, offline field saving, and office review after sync. Use real application screens with sample records. Show the owner-facing report only with the fields and update behaviour the team can demonstrate today.

Page two should answer rollout questions: who trains staff, what happens when a device fails, what needs internet, how existing records will be assessed, and what a small trial would involve. Include Sarbani Associates, Bagdogra, software work since 2000, the approved 20+ estate proof point, and a direct business reply route.

Suggested main line:

> Check hazira and leaf weight before preparing wages.

Suggested invitation:

> See a short demonstration using sample records. We can then discuss whether a review of one division would help your garden.

Use the brief through introductions, office handoffs, requested follow-ups and a small postal test to verified working business addresses. Keep the current rule against attachments in the first cold email.

At the office, change the request:

> Who reviews hazira and leaf-weight reports here? We have a two-page example for that person. What is the best way to send it for review?

Ask for an agreed appointment or forwarding route. Return when there is a named attendee and an agreed time. A delivery receipt does not prove the owner read the material.

The video should demonstrate offline saving, then restore connectivity and show sync and office review. An owner's online dashboard still needs connectivity. Some existing social drafts blur that distinction and should be corrected before use.

## 3. Use LinkedIn to map relationships

Build a working list of 20 eligible estate accounts, ten in North Bengal and ten in Assam. Check exclusions and prior contact first.

For each account, find the current decision-maker where publicly possible, one relevant operational or finance contact, and any credible route through an existing relationship. Search the operating company as well as the garden name. Record role, source and date in the canonical tracker.

Include independent visiting agents, tea consultants and former GardenSuite users who now work elsewhere. Verify current associations. Do not assume a planter title means ownership or an old employer is still current.

Publish two useful product demonstrations a week for four weeks. Topics can include recording a weighing round offline, reviewing records before wages and checking the daily garden report away from the estate. Use actual screens with sample data. Avoid accusations about staff or claims about losses at an unknown estate.

Use relevant Facebook planter groups for the same work, adapted to each discussion and its rules. Ask before moving a public exchange into a private sales conversation. A connection acceptance or a video view is not buying interest.

Paid LinkedIn is a later test. LinkedIn currently requires at least 300 audience members to run a campaign. That is a platform minimum, not proof that enough eligible estate buyers can be reached economically. First check whether the people on the account list are active and responding.

For a later Meta test, choose one demonstrated workflow and one response action. Agree a total loss limit before launching. Measure identifiable estate conversations; cheap views and general tea-consumer enquiries are insufficient. This review does not establish audience availability or ad costs.

## 4. Let buyers see the review before requesting records

The Tea Loss Audit is useful after interest. A stranger requesting attendance and wage files creates another obstacle. Open with a sample demonstration that requires no estate data.

After interest, propose a manual review of a small sample of records the estate agrees to share. Select one question, such as whether attendance, plucking weight and wage inputs agree for one division. Agree access, scope, preparation effort and price before work starts.

Findings should separate missing records, timing differences, corrections and unresolved discrepancies. A discrepancy alone does not prove theft or recoverable savings.

If the review identifies a useful problem, scope a 30-day shadow deployment. Confirm the supported workflow, hardware, training, costs and measures first. Keep the current process running while comparing results. Do not promise future allocation engines, full leaf passports or automatic exception reports as existing features.

## Thirty-day test

These are proposed effort limits and decision thresholds, not conversion forecasts. Kaushik owns the test; design and product help are limited to preparing an accurate demonstration.

| Period | Work | Evidence to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1-3 | Trace the last three customer wins; assemble the brief and video from existing assets | Who opened each sale, what persuaded the buyer, and whether the same route is available |
| Days 4-10 | Have six candidate-introducer conversations, split equally between the two regions | Current estate relationships, one specific introduction sought, and outcome |
| Days 4-14 | Map the 20 accounts; begin relevant LinkedIn and Facebook activity | Verified people, active profiles, responses and agreed forwarding routes |
| Days 11-21 | Hold introduced demonstrations; ask one suitable organiser about a small session | Attendees' roles, stated problem and next step |
| Days 22-30 | Offer a scoped records review where there is interest | Accepted review, requested quote, deferred decision or recorded reason for refusal |

Seek three completed owner/director or authorised-buyer conversations and one accepted records review. If introducers agree but arrange nothing, change the candidates or the ask. If meetings happen but nobody wants a review, examine the problem, offer and price. If reviews happen but no trial follows, examine the findings and rollout burden.

Park unconfirmed repeat visits. Keep cold email as a supporting contact route rather than the main use of founder time. Do not hire, print at scale, buy memberships or launch ads on the strength of this memo alone.
