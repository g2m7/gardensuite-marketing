Status: DRAFT FOR OWNER APPROVAL - DO NOT SEND

Owner: Sarbani Associates

Prepared: 2026-08-16

## Purpose

This package contains the proposed three-email sequence for the 20-estate GardenSuite attendance pilot.

The sequence is built around the questions tea garden buyers ask before considering a new system:

1. Is GardenSuite already used by tea estates near us?
2. Will attendance continue when internet is weak?
3. Who supports us after installation, and what happens when an issue is raised?

The primary reply goal is permission to share a nearby permitted reference and arrange a short product demonstration. The first email does not ask for a long meeting or send an attachment.

## Campaign map

| Touch | Timing | Main idea | Reply goal |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Email 1 | Day 0 | Nearby implementation proof and face-verified hazira | Permission to share the nearest permitted reference |
| Email 2 | Day 5 | Offline field capture and office review | Interest in seeing the field-to-office workflow |
| Email 3 | Day 12 | Setup, training, after-sales support, and a respectful close | Reply `details` or allow the sequence to end |

All emails use plain text, one website link in the signature, no images, no brochure, and no tracking link.

## Claim rules

- Use `20+ tea estates across Assam and North Bengal` as the default proof statement.
- Do not name a client estate unless Sarbani Associates has permission to use that estate as a reference.
- Use `Many estates keep software details private` when explaining why names are not listed publicly.
- Say GardenSuite `helps stop proxy attendance`. Do not claim that it removes all proxy attendance.
- Say the field workflow saves records offline and syncs when connectivity returns.
- Say GardenSuite is built and supported by Sarbani Associates in Bagdogra, Siliguri.
- Do not claim that Sarbani Associates has several offices. Only the Bagdogra, Siliguri base is currently documented.
- Do not promise a routine on-site visit. The location, scope, travel, timing, and charges must be confirmed in the quotation.
- Do not promise a resolution time until the support response proposal has been approved and can be delivered operationally.
